A working proof of concept of the design spec. The plugin's routes, storage and invoice state machine run in this page. The bridge signs every sync with a real Ed25519 key, the plugin verifies it, and a simulated chain mines a block every two minutes of simulated time.

    Why the wallet calls the site, not the other way round

    Most payment plugins reach into a wallet. This one waits for the wallet host to call in, which keeps the plugin's network permission down to two price feeds.

    Fixed defaults, not settings

    Invoice window 30 min · late-payment watch 24 h · buyer data purged 30 days after an invoice is final Tolerance 99.5% · tip dust floor 0.0001 XMR · confirmation tiers: under 100, 100 to 1,000, over 1,000 Settled payments watched until 10 confirmations · a reorged payment has 5 blocks to be mined again · expiry allowance 15 + 3 blocks Required confirmations are locked into each invoice at checkout. Changing the speed here affects new invoices only. Address pool target 10 in this demo (the spec proposes 50) so top-ups and exhaustion are easy to see

      Real in this demo

      • Ed25519 signing and verification through WebCrypto, over x-xmr-ts + "\n" + rawBody
      • The 300-second freshness window and per-invoice seq ordering
      • BigInt atomic-unit math, 99.5% tolerance, confirmation presets, and the six-state invoice machine
      • Hashed per-IP buckets, the address pool, the watch list and the cron sweep

      Simulated

      • The EmDash host and sandbox, modelled as capability checks, per-plugin storage and an allowedHosts gate
      • monerod, wallet-rpc, the network and the buyer's wallet. Addresses and txids are random and not valid on any network
      • Price APIs, with made-up starting rates that drift slightly each minute
      • Demo-only choices: a pool target of 10 instead of 50, and the product title kept on each invoice for the admin table

      Fixed after the first review

      • Settled invoices stay watched until 10 confirmations. A reorg that re-mines the payment shows as re-confirming; a payment that is gone goes to review with an alert
      • Invoices expire only after a sync from past the deadline, and lateness comes from the wallet's mempool time or the block height, not from when the site heard about it
      • Required confirmations are locked at checkout; an admin can raise them on one invoice
      • Time-locked and double-spend-flagged transfers are not counted
